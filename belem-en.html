11:40tarts
The original tart shop Pastéis de Belém Pasteis de Belem R. de Belém 84 · if the takeaway line is long, go inside and sit — there are over a thousand seats through the back rooms and tables turn fast

Open since 1837, using the monks' recipe from the monastery next door — still locked in a secret room that only a handful of people have ever seen. Only this shop may call them pastéis de Belém; everywhere else in the world they're just pastel de nata. Two each, warm, with cinnamon — a warm one is a different food from a cold one. Then it's a two-minute walk to the monastery.

–13:45monastery
Jerónimos Monastery Mosteiro dos Jerónimos Jeronimos Monastery Praça do Império · church is free; the cloister is ticketed — buy the timed ticket online to skip the line

Begun in 1501 and funded by the "pepper money" — Portugal's 5% tax on the India trade — it took a century to build. This is the peak of Manueline architecture: stone carved into ropes, coral and armillary spheres, a whole building rigged like a ship that never sails. Vasco da Gama lies in the free church (first tomb on the left as you enter), but the unmissable part is the ticketed two-storey cloister — honey-coloured stone carved without an inch left plain. The best single ticket in Lisbon. UNESCO-listed.

14:00lunch
Lunch in Belém Enoteca de Belem Travessa do Marta Pinto 10 · in the lane beside the monastery

A small wine bistro hidden in a side lane — Portuguese cooking with good local wine, pleasantly untouristy. Backup: any tasca along Rua de Belém, or just graze and save the real meal for tonight.

15:00riverfront
Monument to the Discoveries → Belém Tower Padrao dos Descobrimentos A riverside walk connects them · search Belem Tower, 15 min on foot

The Monument to the Discoveries: a ship's-prow monument with Henry the Navigator leading Vasco da Gama, Magellan and the rest toward the sea — and look down: the giant compass-rose mosaic maps every Portuguese landfall with its date. Belém Tower: the 1519 fortress guarding the river mouth, the last thing the crews saw as they left. The outside is the sight — the interior is one slow, narrow spiral staircase queue and not worth the time.

16:00coaches
National Coach Museum Museu Nacional dos Coches Museu Nacional dos Coches Av. da Índia 136 · on the walk back from the tower · open until 18:00, one hour is plenty

The most important collection of royal carriages in the world, full stop. One vast hall of 16th–19th-century coaches: fully gilded baroque carving, velvet interiors, allegorical oil paintings on the bodywork — including the three embassy coaches Portugal sent to the Pope in 1716, extravagant enough to stun Rome. Essentially a history of haute couture on wheels — how a crown flexed through craftsmanship. Anyone who's worked in luxury will feel this one professionally. Quiet, cool, exactly an hour.

One more if you have legs left Jardim Botanico Tropical Beside the monastery's east gate · or MAAT (the white wave building on Av. Brasília)

The Tropical Botanical Garden: a century-old palm garden next to the monastery with free-roaming peacocks and almost no tourists — Belém's quietest corner, good for letting the day settle. Or MAAT: the 2016 wave-shaped museum whose roof is a walkable ramp with a great angle on the river and bridge — no ticket needed for the roof. Or neither — go home; tomorrow starts early.

19:00sunset
Rooftop sunset at Park Bar Park Bar Lisboa Calçada do Combro 58 · 5-min walk · the entrance is easy to miss: it's a parking garage — take the lift to the top floor, then one flight of stairs

A rooftop bar hidden on top of a parking garage — one of the locals' favourite sunset spots: full of plants, music on, facing the 25 de Abril bridge and the Cristo Rei across the river. Sunset is ~19:25; arrive between 18:45–19:00 to claim a railing spot. Watch it go dark, then dinner nearby.

Evening option: fado at A Tasca do Chico A Tasca do Chico R. do Diário de Notícias 39, Bairro Alto · 10-min walk · no cover, just order a drink

If you haven't heard fado yet, tonight is the last window (Welcome Dinner tomorrow, wedding Saturday). This is fado vadio — "stray fado" — where the singer might be the guy from next door; hams hang from the ceiling and the walls are covered in football scarves and photos. Arrive by 20:15 for a table; silence during the singing is the house rule. Or stay in — early start tomorrow.

Tonight's dinner: piri piri chicken shortlist

Portugal's national dish — whole chicken spit-roasted over charcoal, brushed with African bird's-eye chilli oil. Mild by default; ask for molho piri-piri to turn it up. Order: half a chicken (meio frango) + fries + tomato rice. Check hours on Maps before heading out.

top pickclassic
Bonjardim, "King of Chickens" Rei dos Frangos Bonjardim Rei dos Frangos Travessa de Santo Antão 11 · 7-min taxi from Park Bar

Open since the 1950s — Lisbon's default answer for piri piri chicken: spit-roasted over charcoal, crisp skin, a little jug of chilli oil to brush on yourself. Easiest move after the sunset.

nearesttakeaway
Frangasqueira Nacional Frangasqueira Nacional Rua da Imprensa Nacional 116 · 7–8 min walk from the flat

A tiny charcoal chicken shop in São Bento with a local queue — grilled to order. Barely any seats; the best move is takeaway: carry one home with a bottle of vinho verde. After a full day on foot, this is the cosiest option.

localbig room
A Valenciana A Valenciana Lisboa Rua Marquês de Fronteira 157 · 10-min taxi

A big 1970s dining room untouched by time — where local families do their chicken dinners. Huge portions, cheap. Go here to see how Lisbon actually eats frango assado.

bonusLagos leg
Save for the Algarve: Ramires (Guia) Restaurante Ramires Guia Guia, 40 min from Lagos · on the Oct 12 Benagil route

Piri piri chicken's birthplace is the town of Guia in the Algarve; this place opened in 1964 and claims to be the original. Do the "pilgrimage version" there and compare it with tonight's.
