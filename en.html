Monday Sep 21 · the city loop

Tram east and uphill from home, then one long walk west and downhill back — no backtracking. About 6 km on foot, nothing has a timed entry, and the only hard deadline is the 18:30 call. Paste the blue boxes straight into Google Maps.

9:30tram
Tram 28 Tram 28E Tram 28 São Bento stop On Calçada da Estrela, 3 min from the flat · direction Martim Moniz, about 25 min

A 1930s wooden tram that is an attraction in its own right — it squeezes through alleys and climbs hills across Lisbon's oldest quarters. Boarding near home means it's still uncrowded. Watch phones and wallets on board — this is the city's pickpocket hotspot. If it's packed, just Uber to the next stop, 10 minutes.

10:00viewpoint
Miradouro de Santa Luzia Miradouro de Santa Luzia Largo Santa Luzia · get off at the "Miradouro Sta. Luzia" stop

Lisbon's most photogenic viewpoint: a bougainvillea-covered pergola, two old azulejo panels (one shows the 1147 siege of the castle), and Alfama's red rooftops running down to the river. Small and lovely — 15 minutes.

10:10viewpoint
Miradouro das Portas do Sol Miradouro das Portas do Sol Largo das Portas do Sol · right next door, 2 min

The wider-angle one — the whole white-and-terracotta old town, two church domes, cruise ships on the river. There's an open-air café if you want an espresso before the climb.

10:30
–12:00castle
São Jorge Castle Castelo de São Jorge Castelo de São Jorge R. de Santa Cruz do Castelo · 10-min uphill walk from the viewpoints · buy tickets at castelodesaojorge.pt to skip the line

The highest point in Lisbon. An 11th-century Moorish castle that Portugal's first king took in 1147 — one of the founding moments of the country. The draw isn't palace interiors (long gone) but the full circuit of ramparts you can climb, 11 watchtowers, the best view in the city, and peacocks wandering the pine gardens. Plenty of benches — today's best spot to do nothing for a while.

12:00
–13:00old town
Alfama alleys + Lisbon Cathedral Sé de Lisboa Sé de Lisboa Largo da Sé · wander downhill from the castle, you'll hit it

Alfama is Lisbon's oldest quarter and the only part that survived the 1755 earthquake, so its Moorish-era maze is intact — laundry overhead, tiled doorways, grilled sardines drifting from corner taverns. Don't use the map; getting lost is the point — keep heading downhill. This is also the birthplace of fado. Halfway down you'll pass the cathedral: the city's oldest church, begun in 1147, built like a fortress — those two bell towers really were defensive towers. 15 minutes inside, it's on the way.

13:00
–14:15lunch
Zé da Mouraria (first choice) Zé da Mouraria Rua João do Outeiro 24, Mouraria · lunch only

An old tasca hidden in Mouraria — the canteen of local office workers, walls covered in fado singer photos. The signature is roast salt cod (bacalhau assado); portions are big enough to share one main between two. Backup: O Velho Eurico (Largo São Cristóvão 3) — an old tavern taken over by a young crew doing petiscos; the octopus and crispy pork belly are great, arrive early. Both are on the walk down from the castle.

14:30square
Praça do Comércio + Rua Augusta Arch Praça do Comércio On the river · walk up through the Arco da Rua Augusta into Baixa

The 1755 earthquake flattened the royal palace; this grand square rose on its site — bright yellow arcades on three sides, the Tagus wide open on the fourth, one of Europe's biggest riverfront squares. In the age of sail, cargo came ashore right here. Have a look, take the photo, then head up the pedestrian street toward Chiado.

15:00
–16:15Chiado
Chiado trio: bookshop · café · ruined convent Livraria Bertrand Chiado Rua Garrett 73 · A Brasileira at Rua Garrett 120 · Convento do Carmo at Largo do Carmo

Livraria Bertrand: open since 1732, Guinness-certified oldest operating bookstore in the world, blue-tiled façade; buy a Pessoa and get the commemorative stamp. A Brasileira: a 1905 café whose bronze statue out front is Pessoa himself, who drank his coffee here daily — a bica standing at the counter is the authentic (and half-price) move. Convento do Carmo: today's most remarkable stop — the 1755 quake brought the church roof down mid-mass, and instead of rebuilding, Lisbon left the Gothic stone skeleton open to the sky for 250+ years. Walking into that roofless nave is unforgettable. If you're flagging, this is the first thing to cut.

16:15
–17:45shops
Príncipe Real + Embaixada Embaixada Lisboa Praça do Príncipe Real 26 · more boutiques along Rua Dom Pedro V

Your own neighbourhood — Lisbon's densest cluster of design and concept stores. The garden's centrepiece is a century-old cedar spread out like an umbrella, with a kiosk café underneath. Embaixada is a 19th-century Moorish-revival palace turned concept store — one Portuguese brand per room (clothes, fragrance, ceramics), and the staircase and courtyard are worth the visit alone. No pressure to finish today; it's next door, any evening works.

18:00home
Walk home, call at 18:30 Travessa de São Plácido 48 · 10 min downhill

19:15sunset
Sunset drink Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara Miradouro de São Pedro de Alcântara R. de São Pedro de Alcântara · 10-min walk · sunset ~19:30

Facing east over Baixa, the castle hill and the river — the whole day's route laid out in front of you. Grab a white port at the kiosk, watch the castle hill turn gold, then head to dinner.
